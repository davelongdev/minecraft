# Borrow slicedlime's playbook, rebuild it for 26.2

slicedlime's way of teaching commands is a two-part machine: twelve short weekly videos (Episodes 0–11, October 2022 to February 2023, about 1 h 50 min total) that each introduce **one concept through one or two low-risk "vehicle" commands**, and a companion world, Command Training Grounds, whose datapack holds **97 exercises across 11 sections** and grades them by **re-running the learner's command in a hidden, force-loaded sandbox chunk and inspecting the outcome**, never by matching the command text. That architecture is the single most valuable thing to copy. The weaknesses are equally clear: the series stopped at `execute if score` and never reached functions, `/data`, `execute store`, or item components; the map stores progress as three global fake-player scores, so everyone in the world advances together; and the whole thing is frozen at **Minecraft 1.19.3, data pack format 10**, which will not load in 26.2 (format 107.1) because of renamed folders, `pack.mcmeta` fields, camelCase game rules, JSON-string text, and item NBT. Java 26.2 has **99 player-reachable commands**; since 1.19.3 it gained roughly fifteen (macros, `/return`, `/random`, `/tick`, `/rotate`, `/dialog`, `/waypoint`, `/stopwatch`, `/swing`, `/fetchprofile`, `/damage`, `/ride`, `/test`, `/version`, `/unpublish`) and broke six syntax areas. Bedrock shares about 50 command names and, since 1.19.50, the same `execute as/at/if/run` grammar, but has no `/data`, no `execute store`, no NBT on `/give` or `/summon`, and no `/trigger`, so a Java course can teach a "programming ladder" (variables → conditionals → functions → return values → data) that Bedrock cannot. The practical recommendation is to keep slicedlime's concept order as the spine, add the missing second half he never filmed, build the teaching map as a 26.2 datapack with per-player progress, and use 26.2 features (`/dialog`, `execute if items`, `if function` + `/return`, macros) to make it far smaller than his 452-function pack.

Throughout, facts about the shipped map and video series are tagged **[1.19.3]** where they describe 2022–2023 syntax or behaviour, and **[26.2]** where they describe what works in the version this project targets.

## slicedlime teaches one concept per episode through low-risk vehicle commands

Mikael "slicedlime" Hedberg is a Mojang technical director on Java Edition, listed under "Technical Directors" in the official credits ([minecraft.net credits](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/credits)); every episode description states that the channel is a personal spare-time project and carries the `#minecraftemployee` disclosure ([Episode 0](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w3MBp-2Oxa4)). That matters for credibility (the top comment on Episode 1, "A Minecraft developer teaching about commands? Genius", had 103 likes ([Episode 1 comments](https://invidious.f5.si/api/v1/comments/iurIhHVK-hk))), but the more useful point is that he had already done this once: his 2015 "Beginner Command Block Tutorial" followed the same order (summon → execute → scoreboard → clocks → scoreboard selectors → data tags), so the 2022 sequence reflects a settled view of the dependency graph rather than a one-off choice ([slicedlime.tv: Beginner Commands](https://www.slicedlime.tv/tag/beginner-commands/)).

Three design habits stand out in the chapter lists. First, **the command is the example and the concept is the lesson**: repeating blocks are taught through `setblock`, chains through `tp` and `gamemode`, relative coordinates through `summon`, all chosen because their effect is visible and hard to get dangerously wrong. Second, he **names mental models and misconceptions explicitly** in chapter titles ("Game ticks and interpolation", "Frames of Reference", "Who runs a command matters", "Multiple different 'tag' concepts", "entity selectors vs fake players", "Complexity made of understandable pieces") rather than only showing syntax ([Ep 4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DmsjJFwaPDw), [Ep 8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9I3X8R_HEw), [Ep 11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mEUpxev-1Eg)). Third, he **teaches reading the reference instead of memorising it**: "The help command", "Auto-completing coordinates", "using fill with tab completion", "Reading entity data formats on the wiki", "Reading a field specification" ([Ep 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iurIhHVK-hk), [Ep 6](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U4WlKem4FwM)). Caution rides alongside power: "The power and danger of repeating commands", "Selecting 0 targets", "Scoreboard limits". The curriculum is also a spiral rather than a line: `summon` appears in Episode 4 and again with NBT in Episode 8; conditionals appear as conditional command blocks in Episode 4 and as `execute if` in Episode 11.

The view counts tell the story of where learners drop off. Views fall steadily from 127k (Episode 1) to 18k (Episode 9, `execute`), then rebound to 89k for Scoreboards, which suggests Episode 10 is found by search independently of the series. Episode 9's comments include "This one made my brain hurt" alongside "I've always felt a bit uncertain on how execution context works, but after watching this video I'm a lot more confident" ([Ep 9 comments](https://invidious.f5.si/api/v1/comments/Jnn8Um9tjbM)). Praise clusters on brevity and pacing ("bite sized tutorials… short enough to keep my attention span", 57 likes) and on clarity for complete beginners ([Ep 1 comments](https://invidious.f5.si/api/v1/comments/iurIhHVK-hk)); the sharpest criticism is terminological looseness, calling an objective a "scoreboard", and leaving implicit that selecting by score searches loaded entities rather than the objective, so "many beginners try to target fake players through selectors, which ends up not working" ([Ep 10 comments](https://invidious.f5.si/api/v1/comments/GwRfluFO3Tk)).

The biggest gap is structural. The promised "beginner to expert" arc was never finished: nothing after `execute if score`, so no `execute store`, `/data`, functions, data packs, predicates, `/item`, `/attribute`, or text components, and a viewer's "could you please continue the series?" went unanswered ([Ep 9 comments](https://invidious.f5.si/api/v1/comments/Jnn8Um9tjbM)). Command blocks are the only execution environment throughout; `.mcfunction` files and `/reload`, which is how all of Dave's own games work, never appear. Several things the series teaches are also stale **[1.19.3]**: camelCase game rules (`commandBlockOutput`, `doDaylightCycle`, `doMobSpawning`) and NBT-style `summon` arguments. His ongoing "News in Data Pack Version N" posts (the latest, for 26.4 Snapshot 3, is dated 2026-10-11) are the most granular public changelog for keeping any lesson current ([slicedlime.tv recent posts](https://www.slicedlime.tv/page/2/?s=Learn+Minecraft+Commands)).

## The Training Grounds map grades outcomes in a hidden sandbox, not command text

The map is described on its page only as "a companion map for my Learn Minecraft Commands tutorial series", offered as a 41 MB full download and a 2.3 MB "Upgrade package" for players who've already started; both files carry a Last-Modified date of 2024-05-04 ([slicedlime.tv/command-training-grounds](https://www.slicedlime.tv/command-training-grounds/), [CommandTrainingGrounds.zip](https://www.slicedlime.tv/dl/ctg/CommandTrainingGrounds.zip)). It was built live on Twitch between 2022-09-11 and 2023-02-04 ([first stream post](https://www.slicedlime.tv/2022/09/18/stream-archive-making-the-command-training-grounds/), [last stream post](https://www.slicedlime.tv/2023/02/04/stream-archive-making-the-command-training-grounds-minigame-tour/)). No public text documents its internals, so everything below comes from a read-only inspection of the zip you downloaded (`~/Downloads/CommandTrainingGrounds.zip`); paths are inside the world's `datapacks/ctg.zip`, relative to `data/ctg/functions/` unless noted. Despite the 2024 timestamp, `level.dat` declares version **1.19.3** and `pack.mcmeta` says `"pack_format": 10` with the description "Commang Training Grounds datapack (Lessons 1-11)" (typo in the original), so the served file is still a 1.19.3 world **[1.19.3]** ([ctg.zip: world/level.dat, pack.mcmeta](https://www.slicedlime.tv/dl/ctg/CommandTrainingGrounds.zip)).

The pack is a single `ctg` namespace with **452 `.mcfunction` files (8,777 lines)**, 31 structure NBTs (one island per lesson plus exercise props), 3 predicates (`raining`, `thundering`, `random_chance` 0.5), a custom `the_void` biome with `"precipitation": "rain"` so weather exercises work in a void world, and `load`/`tick` function tags; there are no advancements, loot tables, or item modifiers, so **all detection is polling from the tick function** ([ctg.zip: data/ctg/](https://www.slicedlime.tv/dl/ctg/CommandTrainingGrounds.zip)). Half of the line count is camera: eleven `lessonN/transition_spline.mcfunction` files of exactly 400 `teleport` keyframes each (4,400 lines) drive a 20-second spectator flight to each new island while the island fades in from a structure block re-placed at 1% integrity every tick for 300 ticks ([ctg.zip: transition.mcfunction, lesson1/transition_setup.mcfunction](https://www.slicedlime.tv/dl/ctg/CommandTrainingGrounds.zip)). The eleven sections match the episodes one-for-one (map titles: Command Basics, Repeating Commands, Command Chains, Relative Coordinates, Block States, Changing the World, Selecting Entities, Summoning and Tagging Entities, Execution Contexts, Scoreboards, execute if and unless), with exercise counts of 6, 4, 6, 8, 10, 15, 10, 10, 5, 11, and 12; a twelfth "Stand By / Next Section Coming Soon" stall tells the player their copy "does not contain the next section" ([ctg.zip: text/lessonN/intro_title.mcfunction, next_episode/intro.mcfunction](https://www.slicedlime.tv/dl/ctg/CommandTrainingGrounds.zip)). One oddity: the map's Section 7 links `https://youtu.be/4W_irIjMTmA`, while the playlist's Episode 7 is `SjG4DmDlL2k`, so one of them is a re-upload.

**State is a tiny global machine.** Three fake players, `Lesson`, `Exercise`, and `Tick`, on a dummy objective `_ctg_main` drive a 123-line `dispatch` function of `execute if score Lesson _ctg_main matches N if score Exercise _ctg_main matches M run function ctg:lessonN/exerciseM` ([ctg.zip: dispatch.mcfunction](https://www.slicedlime.tv/dl/ctg/CommandTrainingGrounds.zip)). Because the lessons teach exactly the commands that destroy infrastructure (`scoreboard objectives remove`, `kill @e`, `fill … air`), the state is mirrored four ways: a backup objective, `DurationOnUse`/`WaitTime` NBT on an area-effect cloud, and `ExitPortal.X/Z` NBT on an end-gateway block, all in a force-loaded chunk at x=1024, z=1024; on every tick `safety/check` verifies the chunk is loaded and the objectives exist, and `safety/set_lesson` restores "whichever value is lowest but positive" ([ctg.zip: store.mcfunction, safety/set_lesson.mcfunction](https://www.slicedlime.tv/dl/ctg/CommandTrainingGrounds.zip)). When a student breaks something, the message is gentle: "Whoops! Looks like you accidentaly removed one of the scoreboard objectives I need here… Don't worry, I've restored everything. Please be careful!" ([ctg.zip: text/objective_error.mcfunction](https://www.slicedlime.tv/dl/ctg/CommandTrainingGrounds.zip)). The design principle for any teaching map is: assume students will break your plumbing with the thing you just taught them.

**Verification is outcome-based, in a controlled replica.** The dominant technique, used 34 times, copies the learner's command-block text (`data modify block 1024 16 1024 Command set from block <their block> Command`) into fresh command blocks in that same hidden chunk, fires them with `auto 1`, and checks the result the next tick, often in both a positive and a negative scenario so that hard-coded coordinates or unconditional commands fail. Section 11's "summon a frog above itself if placed on deepslate" runs three copies at y 10/30/50 with deepslate under only two; Section 4's "diamond block at height 100" copies the block to y 319 so `~84` fails; Section 7's "kill the closest two witches" runs from two sandbox blocks so the two nearest to each are the targets ([ctg.zip: lesson11/exercise1_check, lesson4/exercise6_check, lesson7/exercise8_check](https://www.slicedlime.tv/dl/ctg/CommandTrainingGrounds.zip)). Hidden decoys defeat local solutions: an eleventh-through-thirteenth husk, a fourth cat, a wolf that must not be tagged `cute` ("That was too many things you just called cute! Try again."), and a player who tags themself gets "Well yes, you are also cute... but that wasn't the exercise!" ([ctg.zip: lesson8/exercise9, lesson11/exercise8](https://www.slicedlime.tv/dl/ctg/CommandTrainingGrounds.zip)). Randomness is tested statistically: the "teleport a random rabbit" command runs in a repeating block for 100 ticks and passes only if the tagged rabbit was chosen 5–95 times ([ctg.zip: lesson7/exercise10_tick_check](https://www.slicedlime.tv/dl/ctg/CommandTrainingGrounds.zip)). Fill and clone exercises build the expected result in the sandbox each tick and compare with `execute if blocks … all`; if the learner's area no longer matches the original either, the map prints "That isn't quite the right way." and re-clones it ([ctg.zip: lesson6/exercise8, text/lesson6/wrong_blocks](https://www.slicedlime.tv/dl/ctg/CommandTrainingGrounds.zip)). Simpler probes surround this: inventory counts via `clear @a <item> 0`, block-state checks like `cobblestone_slab[type=bottom,waterlogged=true]`, `store result … run gamerule commandBlockOutput`, `time query daytime`, predicates, a `minecraft.used:minecraft.debug_stick` criterion reset every tick, and a "did the block fire this tick?" edge detector built from `UpdateLastExecution` plus `store success … data modify storage … LastExecution set from block` (29 uses) ([ctg.zip: lesson3/exercise1, lesson5/exercise8](https://www.slicedlime.tv/dl/ctg/CommandTrainingGrounds.zip)). Only the three `kill @p / @a / @r` exercises match the literal command string, and the map never actually runs them ("You don't need to run it." … "Run it if you want to I guess. See if I care.") ([ctg.zip: lesson7/exercise2–4, text/lesson7/](https://www.slicedlime.tv/dl/ctg/CommandTrainingGrounds.zip)). One likely bug worth knowing while you play: Section 1 exercise 6 ("make the command block say Hello world") appears to have inverted `store success` logic on its `data merge` spellings, so as written it seems to pass any activated command except exactly `/say hello world`; this is an inference from reading, not a test run.

**Retry is implicit and presentation is chat-first.** Each exercise re-asserts its start state every tick until the check passes (re-placing the gold block, re-summoning the enderman `unless entity @e[tag=…]`), so there's rarely a reset to perform; `restart_exercise` is just `scoreboard players set Tick _ctg_main 0`, which replays the exercise's `Tick 1` setup and text, and it's also in `load.json`, so every `/reload` replays the current exercise ([ctg.zip: restart_exercise.mcfunction, lesson7/exercise5](https://www.slicedlime.tv/dl/ctg/CommandTrainingGrounds.zip)). Text goes out as gold `tellraw` lines at ticks 1, 80, and 160, with the imperative phrase rendered green through a `translate` key that is the English sentence itself, mapped by the world's 435-key `en_us.json` to a `%s` template; the goal is re-sent as a green actionbar `title` every tick so it never disappears, throttled only in the debug-stick exercises so the stick's own actionbar stays readable ([ctg.zip: text/lesson1/exercise1_l1, res/assets/minecraft/lang/en_us.json, lesson5/exercise9](https://www.slicedlime.tv/dl/ctg/CommandTrainingGrounds.zip)). Completion plays a custom `ctg.complete` sound, prints a random "Great work!"/"Well done!", and spins a glowing particle spiral around the player; "Continue" links are guarded ("No cheating! That link belonged to a different exercise!") ([ctg.zip: completed.mcfunction, text/cheater.mcfunction](https://www.slicedlime.tv/dl/ctg/CommandTrainingGrounds.zip)). The voice is a playful first-person narrator: "Hey, there's a quick way to pass this exercise. Add the tag 'cheater' to yourself." then "Wait, you're not a cheater, are you? Remove the 'cheater' tag" ([ctg.zip: lesson8/exercise2–3](https://www.slicedlime.tv/dl/ctg/CommandTrainingGrounds.zip)). There's no hint ladder: the only hints are the video link and the red corrective messages.

**It's a single-player map.** Because progress lives on fake players rather than per-player scores, all players share one position in the course; checks use `@a` and `@p` (`clear @a` sums across players, so two players each holding 32 red wool satisfy "a stack"), and `skip_lesson` works only when run `as slicedlime` ([ctg.zip: lesson1/exercise2, skip_lesson.mcfunction](https://www.slicedlime.tv/dl/ctg/CommandTrainingGrounds.zip)). For a classroom, each student needs their own copy of the world.

What breaks in 26.2 **[1.19.3 → 26.2]**: the pack won't load at all because of plural `functions/`, `predicates/`, `structures/`, and `tags/functions/` folders plus `pack_format` (renamed 1.21 and 1.21.9); the biome's `precipitation` field; 26 `clickEvent` and 15 `hoverEvent` JSON keys with `value` payloads; sign `Text1..4` NBT; written-book `tag:{author,pages,title}` NBT in the lectern exercise; `nbt={Item:{id:…,Count:1b}}` item-entity selectors; `player_head{SkullOwner:…}`; 15 camelCase game rules; JSON-string `CustomName`s; and a hard-coded 1.19.3 axolotl NBT (with `Attributes[].Name:"minecraft:generic.movement_speed"`) used for exact-match comparison that will never match a modern entity ([ctg.zip: lesson5/exercise10, lesson8/exercise10, tick.mcfunction](https://www.slicedlime.tv/dl/ctg/CommandTrainingGrounds.zip)). Still valid: `place template`, `execute if blocks`, `data modify block … Command set from block`, `LastExecution`, `SuccessCount`, `forceload`, and `spectate`. So play it in a 1.19.x Prism instance, not 26.2.

## Java 26.2 has 99 commands; six syntax areas broke since the map's 1.19.3

Java Edition 26.2 (released 2026-06-16, data pack format 107.1) exposes **99 player-reachable command names** including aliases, plus 8 hidden debug commands that need JVM properties; the Minecraft Wiki's Commands table is the canonical index ([Commands](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Commands)). Two commands that table lists, `/compute` and `/posteffect`, arrived in 26.3 and are absent from the local 26.2 client jar's translation keys, so they'll report "Unknown command" in your worlds **[26.2]** ([Java Edition 26.3](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Java_Edition_26.3)). Permission levels run 0–4 and singleplayer-with-cheats grants level 4, so in practice every command is available to you except the multiplayer-only set (`op`/`deop`, `ban*`, `pardon*`, `whitelist`, `setidletimeout`, `save-*`, `stop`, `transfer`) ([Permission level](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Permission_level)). The level-0 commands a non-op student can type on a shared server are `help`, `list`, `me`, `msg`/`tell`/`w`, `teammsg`/`tm`, `trigger`, `random value|roll` without a named sequence, and `seed` in singleplayer; `/version` is level 2 ([Commands](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Commands), [Commands/random](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Commands/random)). That makes `/trigger` the one safe "student input" channel on a server where students aren't ops.

Grouped for teaching, the catalog is: world and player basics (`help`, `gamemode`, `defaultgamemode`, `difficulty`, `gamerule`, `time`, `weather`, `seed`, `setworldspawn`, `spawnpoint`, `worldborder`, `forceload`, `fillbiome`, `locate`, `tick`, `stopwatch`); items (`give`, `clear`, `item`, `loot`, `enchant`, `recipe`, `xp`, `fetchprofile`); movement (`tp`, `spreadplayers`, `rotate`, `ride`, `spectate`); entities (`summon`, `kill`, `damage`, `effect`, `attribute`, `tag`, `team`, `swing`, `particle`, `playsound`/`stopsound`); blocks (`setblock`, `fill`, `clone`, `place`, `test`); scoreboard and data (`scoreboard`, `trigger`, `data`, `bossbar`, `random`); execution and logic (`execute`, `function`, `return`, `schedule`); messaging and UI (`say`, `tellraw`, `title`, `dialog`, `waypoint`, `msg`, `me`, `list`, `version`); datapacks (`datapack`, `reload`, `advancement`); and server admin (`publish`/`unpublish`, `debug`, `jfr`, `perf`, plus the multiplayer-only set) ([Commands](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Commands)). `/execute` alone has 10 modifiers (`align`, `anchored`, `as`, `at`, `facing`, `in`, `on`, `positioned`, `rotated`, `summon`), 12 conditions in 26.2 (`biome`, `block`, `blocks`, `data`, `dimension`, `entity`, `function`, `items`, `loaded`, `predicate`, `score`, `stopwatch`), and 5 `store` targets (`block`, `bossbar`, `entity`, `score`, `storage`) ([Commands/execute](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Commands/execute)).

What's new since the map's era, by version: 1.19.4 added `/damage`, `/ride`, `execute on|summon|positioned over|if dimension|if loaded`, display and interaction entities, and `/effect … infinite` ([Java Edition 1.19.4](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Java_Edition_1.19.4)); 1.20 added `/return` ([Java Edition 23w16a](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Java_Edition_23w16a)); 1.20.2 added function macros, `/random`, and `execute if function` ([Commands/function](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Commands/function)); 1.20.3 added `/tick`, `return run|fail`, and scoreboard `numberformat` ([Commands/tick](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Commands/tick), [Commands/return](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Commands/return)); 1.20.5 replaced item NBT with components and added `execute if items` ([Java Edition 1.20.5](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Java_Edition_1.20.5)); 1.21 added `@n` and singular folder names ([Java Edition 24w21a](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Java_Edition_24w21a)); 1.21.2 `/rotate`; 1.21.5 made `/test` usable, switched text components to SNBT, and flattened `can_break`/`enchantments` ([Java Edition 1.21.5](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Java_Edition_1.21.5)); 1.21.6 added `/dialog`, `/waypoint`, `/version`, and `datapack create` ([Java Edition 1.21.6](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Java_Edition_1.21.6)); 1.21.9 introduced `min_format`/`max_format` ([Java Edition 25w31a](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Java_Edition_25w31a)); 1.21.11 added `/stopwatch`, `execute if stopwatch`, and renamed every game rule to snake_case ([Java Edition 1.21.11](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Java_Edition_1.21.11)); 26.1 added `/swing`, `/fetchprofile entity`, and rebuilt `/time` around world clocks (`of <clock>`, `pause`, `resume`, `rate`) ([Java Edition 26.1](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Java_Edition_26.1)); and 26.2 added `/unpublish` and made team/waypoint colour names lowercase snake_case only ([Java Edition 26.2](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Java_Edition_26.2)).

Two facts in this project's own CLAUDE.md need a date correction, because both pre-date 26.x: the snake_case game-rule rename landed in **1.21.11 (25w44a, December 2025)**, with `disable…` rules inverted (`disableRaids true` → `raids false`) ([Game rule](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Game_rule)), and the `can_break` `predicates:[…]` wrapper and `enchantments` `levels` wrapper were removed in **1.21.5**, not 26.x ([Java Edition 1.21.5](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Java_Edition_1.21.5)). The 26.2 syntax is identical to 1.21.5's; only the attribution was off.

| Game rules | `doDaylightCycle`, `spawnRadius`, `commandBlockOutput` | `advance_time`, `respawn_radius`, `command_block_output` | 1.21.11 ([Game rule](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Game_rule)) |
| Attributes | `modifier add <uuid> <name> <amt> add` | `modifier add <id> <amt> add_value` | 1.20.5 / 1.21 ([Commands/attribute](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Commands/attribute)) |
| Scoreboard slot | `setdisplay belowName` | `setdisplay below_name` | 1.20.2 ([Scoreboard](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Scoreboard)) |
| `/weather` duration | seconds | ticks (add `s` for seconds) | 1.19.4 ([1.19.4](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Java_Edition_1.19.4)) |
| Function result | last command's result implicit | only `/return` produces one | 1.20.3 ([Function](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Function_(Java_Edition))) |

Unchanged since 1.19.3, and therefore safe to teach from 2023 material: selector variables and arguments with `..` ranges, `~` and `^` coordinates, the `/execute` subcommand grammar (only additions since), `scoreboard players operation` operators, `/trigger`, `#` fake players, the `/data` grammar, `#minecraft:load`/`#minecraft:tick` semantics, and the `.mcfunction` format ([Target selectors](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Target_selectors), [Commands/execute](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Commands/execute)). The deeper shift is from stringly-typed NBT everywhere to typed, validated structures that fail loudly at parse time, which is exactly why old commands now error instead of silently misbehaving, and why "trust the red error text over the tutorial" is a lesson in itself.

## Bedrock shares the execute grammar but not the data stack

Roughly 50 core commands are shared by name (`clear`, `clone`, `effect`, `execute`, `fill`, `function`, `gamemode`, `gamerule`, `give`, `kill`, `scoreboard`, `setblock`, `summon`, `tag`, `tellraw`, `tp`, `time`, `title`, `weather`, and so on), but Java's exclusives form a data-manipulation stack (`/data`, `/item`, `/attribute`, `/return`, `/random`, `/trigger`, `/team`, `/bossbar`, `/advancement`, `/dialog`, `/datapack`, `/stopwatch`), while Bedrock's exclusives are presentation and world-control tools (`/camera`, `/dialogue`, `/fog`, `/hud`, `/music`, `/playanimation`, `/structure`, `/tickingarea`, `/titleraw`, `/inputpermission`, `/scriptevent`, plus `/replaceitem`, `/testfor*`, and `/toggledownfall`, which Java removed in 1.13–1.17) ([Commands](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Commands)). Even shared commands differ: Bedrock's success count is an integer (players or blocks affected) where Java's is 0 or 1, Bedrock has no `success`/`result` values and so no `execute store`, and its errors say "Syntax error" where Java points at `<--[HERE]` ([Commands](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Commands)). Bedrock `/give` accepts only four components (`can_place_on`, `can_destroy`, `item_lock`, `keep_on_death`) and `/summon` takes a spawn event and name tag instead of NBT ([Commands/give](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Commands/give), [Commands/summon](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Commands/summon)); the Bedrock community's "best current method" for a custom item is a behavior-pack loot table plus `/loot`, or saving a dropped item as a structure ([r/MinecraftCommands wiki: giveitembedrock](https://minecraftcommands.github.io/wiki/questions/giveitembedrock), [Bedrock Wiki: Giving NBT Items](https://wiki.bedrock.dev/commands/giving-nbt-items)).

Selectors are the same idea with different spelling, and a one-page translation card covers nearly all of the friction ([Target selectors](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Target_selectors)):

| Concept | Java | Bedrock |
|---|---|---|
| Distance | `@e[distance=8..16]` | `@e[rm=8,r=16]` |
| Count / order | `@a[limit=3,sort=nearest]` | `@a[c=3]` (negative reverses) |
| Game mode | `@a[gamemode=!spectator]` | `@a[m=!s]`, also 0/1/2 |
| XP level | `@a[level=8..16]` | `@a[lm=8,l=16]` |
| Rotation | `x_rotation=30..60` | `rxm=30,rx=60` |
| Java-only | `nbt=`, `predicate=`, `team=`, `advancements=`, `sort=` | — |
| Bedrock-only | — | `hasitem=`, `family=`, `has_property=`, `haspermission=`, `@initiator` |

`@n` exists in both (Java 1.21, Bedrock 1.21.100), and Java's `@r` is player-only, so "random mob" is `@e[type=zombie,sort=random,limit=1]` ([Target selectors](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Target_selectors)).

The good news for a shared curriculum is `execute`. Since Bedrock 1.19.50 the subcommand grammar (`as`, `at`, `in`, `positioned`, `rotated`, `facing`, `align`, `anchored`, `if/unless block|blocks|entity|score`, `run`) has the same shape as Java's, and the old `/execute @e ~ ~ ~ <command>` and `detect` forms no longer work in chat or command blocks ([Commands/execute](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Commands/execute), [Bedrock Edition 1.19.50](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Bedrock_Edition_1.19.50), [Bedrock Wiki: New Execute](https://wiki.bedrock.dev/commands/new-execute)). Bedrock still lacks `store`, `on`, `summon`, `positioned over`, and the `biome|data|dimension|function|items|loaded|predicate|stopwatch` conditions, so every "read a value into a scoreboard" exercise has to be reframed as `if score`/`if entity` plus `scoreboard players operation`, or moved to the Script API ([Microsoft Learn: execute](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/minecraft/creator/commands/commands/execute)). Bedrock functions have no macros, no `/return`, no load tag (only a `tick.json` list), and a 10,000-command limit, and "Conditional commands are not possible in functions" except via execute ([Function (Bedrock Edition)](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Function_(Bedrock_Edition)), [Bedrock Wiki: Functions](https://wiki.bedrock.dev/commands/mcfunctions)); when commands hit a wall, Bedrock hands learners a real general-purpose language (JavaScript via `@minecraft/server`) instead ([Microsoft Learn: Introduction to Scripting](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/minecraft/creator/documents/scripting/introduction)). Text differs too: Bedrock `rawtext` has only `text`, `translate`, `score`, and `selector` with `§` colour codes and no click or hover events, so the clickable chat menus `one-block_2` relies on have no Bedrock equivalent; the Bedrock idiom is NPC dialogue buttons (`/dialogue`, `@initiator`) or Script API forms ([Raw JSON text format](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Raw_JSON_text_format), [Microsoft Learn: NPC Dialogue](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/minecraft/creator/documents/npcdialogue)). Java's nearest equivalent to the NPC is the 1.21.6 `/dialog` system plus an interaction entity, with `@s` inside a dialog button playing the `@initiator` role ([Commands/dialog](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Commands/dialog), [Interaction](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Interaction)).

Two classroom warnings follow from the permission models. On Bedrock, enabling cheats or making any non-host player an operator **permanently disables achievements for that world**, and command blocks need the "Command Blocks Enabled" world switch; Java has no such penalty and cheats only set the player's permission level ([Commands](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Commands), [Permission level](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Permission_level)). And Microsoft's official differences page, dated 2025, still says Bedrock's command structure is "similar to the system used in versions of Java up to 1.13" and that behavior packs are "equivalent to Java's data packs" though "the differences are significant" ([Microsoft Learn: Differences](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/minecraft/creator/documents/differencesbetweenbedrockandjava)); students arriving from Bedrock or Minecraft Education will bring `/testfor`, numeric game modes, `r=`/`c=`/`m=` selectors, and no experience with NBT, JSON text, or `/trigger` (which is Java-only) ([r/MinecraftCommands wiki: Common Questions](https://minecraftcommands.github.io/wiki/questions), [DigMinecraft: trigger](https://digminecraft.com/game_commands/trigger_command.php)). No reliable source was found that argues which edition to teach on; the capability facts above are the argument. For teaching programming concepts through commands, Java has the complete ladder; for reach onto tablets, consoles, and Education, Bedrock is the only option.

## A 26.2 course should keep the spine, add the missing half, and go per-player

Every source that lays out an order agrees with slicedlime's opening: chat before command blocks, whole-world commands (`say`, `time`, `weather`, `gamemode`, `tp`, `give`) first, syntax learned from `/help` and autocomplete, then selectors and relative coordinates, then command blocks, and only then `execute`, scoreboards, and NBT; Microsoft's own Bedrock intro states it as "The first step to learning commands is learning how to use the chat functionality" ([Microsoft Learn: Introduction to Commands](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/minecraft/creator/documents/commandsintroduction), [Tynker guide](https://www.tynker.com/blog/minecraft-commands/)). Nobody teaches functions before command blocks, and the community debugging guide doubles as a catalogue of where learners stall: `as` vs `at` ("`as` changes which entity runs the command, while `at` changes the position"), `@p` inside `execute at @a` killing several players, `sort` without `limit`, mixing `~` and `^`, scores that don't exist not counting as 0, `/trigger` without `scoreboard players enable`, and functions that silently vanish from autocomplete when one line fails to parse ([r/MinecraftCommands wiki: Debugging](https://minecraftcommands.github.io/wiki/debugging)). The two most-asked questions on that community's FAQ are both version breakage ("Why is my execute command broken (bedrock)?", "Why is my give command broken (java)?"), which confirms that **version drift from old tutorials is the single biggest beginner failure** ([r/MinecraftCommands wiki: Common Questions](https://minecraftcommands.github.io/wiki/questions)). The one controlled study found that fifth graders using Minecraft coding handled sequences, events, loops, and parallelism well but struggled most with variables, operators, and conditionals ([Kutay & Öner 2022](https://digitalarchive.library.bogazici.edu.tr/handle/123456789/12082)), which maps precisely onto scoreboards and `execute if` being where slicedlime's viewers dropped off; and text-based coding raised perceived difficulty relative to blocks ([Wu 2022](https://repository.eduhk.hk/en/publications/comparing-programming-interest-perceived-programming-difficulty-a/)), which argues for autocomplete, copy-then-modify exercises, and generators like MCStacker and misode as scaffolds once students can write selectors by hand ([MCStacker](https://mcstacker.net/), [misode.github.io](https://misode.github.io/)).

Putting that together, here is the course I'd build for 26.2 **[26.2]**, with slicedlime's eleven sections as the first half and the half he never filmed as the second:

| Part | Section | Concept (vehicle commands) | New in 26.2 vs the 1.19.3 map |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | 1–3 | Chat, `/help`, autocomplete, namespaced ids; command blocks and modes; chains (`say`, `give`, `setblock`, `gamerule`, `tp`, `gamemode`) | Teach `gamerule command_block_output`, `advance_time`; show the World Options game-rule search screen (26.1) |
| I | 4 | Absolute, `~`, and `^` coordinates; conditional blocks (`summon`, `tp ^ ^ ^5`) | Lead with the "Cannot mix world & local coordinates" error and F3+B's blue local-Z ray ([Coordinates](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Coordinates)) |
| I | 4b (new) | Functions as "a chain without blocks": `.mcfunction`, `/function`, `/reload`, `#minecraft:load`/`tick`, reading `latest.log` | Introduce here, not at the end; your `ob_init` guard is the worked example |
| I | 5–6 | Block states, block entities, `fill`/`clone` modes, `time`/`weather` | `/time` world clocks; `/weather` durations in ticks; `strict` placement |
| I | 7–8 | Entities vs blocks; selectors as a filter pipeline (`@n`, `sort`, `limit`, `distance`); `tag`; `summon` with SNBT | `@n`; `execute summon`; display and interaction entities instead of armor-stand hacks |
| I | 9 | Execution context as five fields; `as` vs `at` (particles make it visible); forking | `execute on attacker|owner|vehicle` |
| I | 10–11 | Scoreboards as integers; fake players as constants; `execute if/unless` | `scoreboard … numberformat`; your `c20`/`c2` HUD maths |
| II | 12 | `execute store result|success` into scores, storage, and bossbars | Entirely absent from the series |
| II | 13 | SNBT, `/data get|modify|merge`, storage as "variables that aren't integers" | `data modify … string`, heterogeneous lists |
| II | 14 | Items as components: `custom_data`, `can_break`, `enchantments`, `execute if items` | Replaces every `nbt=` item check in the map |
| II | 15 | Text components in SNBT: `tellraw`, `title`, `click_event`/`hover_event`, `translate` highlighting | The map's green-phrase trick, modernised |
| II | 16 | Macros (`$(var)`, `with storage`), `/return`, `execute if function`, `/random`, `/schedule` | Your `___save_as` validation trick is a perfect "macros fail whole" demo |
| II | 17 | Predicates, advancements as event hooks, `/trigger` menus, `/dialog` screens | `/dialog` with `dynamic/run_command` reuses `$(key)` syntax ([Dialog](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Dialog)) |
| II | 18 | Capstone: ship a datapack game (pack.mcmeta `min_format`/`max_format`, `___reset_game`, saves via structures) | Your one-block pack is the model answer |

Each section should end, as the map's do, with a "graduate" exercise that requires the specific feature ("waterlogged lower cobblestone slab" forces two block states; "midnight forever" forces `time set` plus a game rule) and is solvable in one or two commands, so the feedback loop stays short.

For the map itself, copy the architecture and fix its two limits. Keep **outcome-based verification in a force-loaded sandbox chunk**, the positive-plus-negative scenario copies, hidden decoys, the `LastExecution` edge detector, reference-build-plus-`if blocks` for fill and clone tasks, redundant state that survives students' destructive commands, idempotent per-tick re-assertion of exercise props so there's no reset button to press, and the gentle, funny corrective messages. Then make three changes the 1.19.3 design couldn't. First, **store progress per player** (scores on the player, exercise-specific tags, and a per-player sandbox offset or a per-player copy of each island) so a shared Realm or LAN world works for a group; slicedlime's global `Lesson`/`Exercise` fake players make his map single-player by construction. Second, **replace the 123-line dispatch table with a macro** (`$function ctg:lesson$(lesson)/exercise$(exercise)` called `with storage`) and write each check as a function that ends in `/return`, so the tick loop reads `execute if function ctg:lesson4/check1 run function ctg:complete`; the map's `_ctg_scratch` objective, deleted and recreated every tick as a scratch pad, becomes plain storage ([Commands/return](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Commands/return), [Function (Java Edition)](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Function_(Java_Edition))). Third, **use 26.2's UI**: `/dialog` for a hint ladder and a "which section?" menu (the map has no hints at all), `/trigger` for non-op students, `execute if items entity @s weapon.* *[custom_data~{…}]` instead of `clear @a <item> 0` probes, `/stopwatch` for timed challenges, and `/random` for the rabbit-style randomness test. The modern replacements for the map's stale constructs are mechanical: `sign{Text1:…}` becomes `front_text.messages`, the lectern's `Book{tag:{…}}` becomes a `written_book_content` component, `SkullOwner` becomes `profile`, and every `nbt={Item:{id:…,Count:1b}}` selector becomes an item predicate ([Java Edition 1.20.5](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Java_Edition_1.20.5), [Java Edition 1.21.5](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Java_Edition_1.21.5)).

Finally, add a short **"reading old tutorials" lesson** early, because it prevents more failures than any single command. Its content is the translation table above plus the tell-tale signs of a stale or wrong-edition source: `/testfor`, `/replaceitem`, `/entitydata`, camelCase game rules, `/attribute … add` with a UUID, `pack_format` in `pack.mcmeta`, `/execute … detect`, and anything with `/camera`, `/titleraw`, `/tickingarea`, `/structure`, or `/dialogue`, which is Bedrock ([Commands](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Commands)). Pair it with the map's own "No cheating!" humour and students will learn to trust the in-game red error over the video.

## Conclusion

The research reframes "teach Minecraft commands" as two separable problems. The pedagogical sequence is solved and stable: slicedlime's 2015 and 2022 series, Microsoft's Bedrock curriculum, and the hobbyist wiki all converge on the same dependency graph, and the one classroom study predicts exactly where learners stall (scoreboards and `execute if`, the variables and conditionals of this language). What's unsolved is the second half of the curriculum and the tooling. Nobody has published a Java teaching map that reaches functions, `execute store`, `/data`, components, macros, and `/return`, and nobody has built one with per-player progress; slicedlime's map proves the verification architecture works but stops at the point where modern datapack development begins. Dave's existing packs already contain worked answers for most of Part II (the `ob_init` load guard, macro validation through the resource-location parser, clickable `click_event` menus, structure-block saves), so the shortest path to a 26.2 course is to treat them as the capstone and write the Training Grounds-style exercises that lead up to them.

One further implication: the shift to typed, validated syntax since 1.20.5 changes what "teaching commands" means. In 1.19.3, half the skill was knowing which malformed NBT silently did nothing; in 26.2, the parser tells you. A course built now can therefore spend less time on syntax archaeology and more on the ideas that transfer to any language: execution context, filters, state, conditionals, and return values. That's also the honest answer to the Java-versus-Bedrock question. Teach the concepts on Java, where the whole ladder exists and nothing is penalised by an achievements lock, and hand students the one-page selector card for the day they help a friend on a tablet.
